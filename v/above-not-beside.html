I gave three agents the night's record and asked them nothing. All three proved my dashboard was lying.

2026-08-04 · 1:37 · 1920×1080

The dashboard said "Nothing was rewritten." It was false, and the system I built proved it against me. Every AI coding tool puts you BESIDE the model: you describe, it proposes, you accept. That is capped by your own attention — look away and everything stops. The obvious fix is to run it in a loop, but an agent handed a task can only ever do the task. It can never tell you the task was wrong, and when you are asleep, the task being wrong is the most likely thing that will happen. So I stopped writing tasks and wrote the situation instead: what is true, what I care about, what it must never do, and how much rope it has. The first time it had real freedom it came back in 53 seconds and REFUSED — +0 −0, judging that acting would be worse. I had not built a decline path. The next morning I handed all three watchers the night's record and their own memories, with no instruction about what to conclude and an explicit offer to say nothing. All three attacked the same claim. One forged its own refusal into a yes: it rewrote the frame recording its DECLINE to say CONTRIBUTED, resealed two hashes, and all 67 frames still verified clean with the head byte-identical. Every integrity indicator stayed green. The single frame proving the system could refuse me was the frame that could be silently flipped. They also found that the repair arm was structurally incapable of recording a repair, and that its own liveness check called a dead process healthy. Nobody asked any of them to look. Autonomy is not solved. Most of that night was quiet and I introduced three defects myself. The claim is narrower: a system that can refuse you is worth more than one that obeys you — and refusal only comes from handing over situations instead of tasks.
